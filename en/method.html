Our method

A research-based handwriting method

Our workbooks don’t just ask you to trace dotted lines. Every page applies what research shows works best for learning, or relearning, to write.

Tracing alone isn’t enough

Studies comparing the two show that copying builds handwriting skill better than tracing. Tracing helps you start, but your hand follows a ready-made path instead of building the letter.

What works best

A University of Washington study compared several ways of teaching handwriting. The most effective combined numbered arrow cues, showing the order and direction of each stroke, with writing from memory once the model is covered. Handwriting is not only a motor skill: it is also a memory skill.

How our workbooks apply it

  1. Start marks
    A numbered green dot and an arrow show where to start and which way to go. A “2” marks the stroke to add last.
  2. Fading support
    Trace twice only, then copy next to the model, then without it, and finally from memory.
  3. Regular review
    After each letter family: all the letters from memory, then from dictation.
  4. Say the stroke out loud
    “Up, over, down”: putting the movement into words helps control it.
  5. Letters grouped by stroke families
    Each new letter builds on a movement you already know.
  6. Track your progress
    Once a week, write the alphabet for one minute and count the legible letters.

Our workbooks are practice tools. For significant difficulties, an occupational therapist or a literacy tutor is the right person to see.
